Street-based neighborhood perception
Transportation and Urban Studio E
Dataset description | Prepared 10 November 2026

Context
-------
This case study examines which streets people regard as belonging to a named neighborhood. It links individual cognitive maps to street location, geographic boundaries and visual streetscape features. The outcome is a binary inclusion judgment for each person-street pair; people can include many streets in their perceived neighborhood. [1, 2]

Data Collection
---------------
On-street surveys were conducted on 20, 21 and 23 October 2020 in Kiyosumi-Shirakawa and Kiba, Tokyo. Respondents drew the area they associated with each neighborhood name on a map. The original study collected 25 residents and 25 visitors in each neighborhood, or 100 respondents in total. Reference points were near the corresponding metro stations and major parks. [1]

The full research dataset crosses 50 respondents with 258 streets in Kiyosumi-Shirakawa and 473 streets in Kiba, yielding 36,550 person-street observations. The local class sample street_perception_sample.csv contains 5,000 rows and 42 columns, covering all 100 respondents and all 731 city-street combinations.

Choice outcome and identifiers
------------------------------
Field / key | Meaning
included | 1 = the respondent includes the street in the perceived neighborhood; 0 = does not include it.
(city, resident, person) | Respondent key. person = 1-25 is reused across cities and resident/visitor groups. This composite key identifies 100 people.
(city, street) | Street key. Street numbers are reused across the two study areas.
(city, resident, person, street) | Observation key: unique for all 5,000 local rows.

A row is a binary judgment, not one member of a mutually exclusive set of candidate streets. Summing included within a respondent does not generally give one. A binary logit is a natural starting point; panel and spatial models can account for dependence across judgments. [2]

--------------

Variables: street attributes and outcome
----------------------------------------
Name | Description / coding
city | Kiyosumi (Kiyosumi-Shirakawa) or Kiba.
street / person | Street ID / respondent number within city and resident/visitor group. Use the composite keys on page 1.
included | Binary neighborhood-inclusion response (1 yes, 0 no).
distance_m / distance_km | Network shortest-path distance from the neighborhood reference point to the nearer endpoint of the street, in metres / kilometres. These are two units of the same measure. [1]
tree / building / road /; sky / other | Percentages of image pixels classified as trees, buildings, road, sky and other features. Values use a 0-100 scale and sum to 100 in every local row.
along_park | Indicator for a street along a park.
inner_river_0 | Indicator for a street inside the reference river boundary.
inner_area | Indicator for a street inside the address-based neighborhood boundary.
inner_avenue_0 | Indicator for a street inside the reference avenue boundary.
cos_simil | Cosine similarity of the street's visual composition to the neighborhood reference composition. Larger values mean greater similarity.
euclid_dist | Euclidean distance between visual-composition vectors. Larger values mean greater visual dissimilarity.

-------------------------------------
Name | Description / coding
resident / visitor | Indicators for the two survey groups. Residents lived within the survey basemap area; visitors did not. These sum to 1.
male / female | Gender indicators. These sum to 1 in the local sample.
age_0_20 / age_30 /; age_40 / age_50 / age_60+ | Age-band indicators: below 30, thirties, forties, fifties, and 60 or older.
Lower_40 / Upper_40 | Age below 40 / age 40 or older. These duplicate aggregated information from the age-band fields.
under_year / year_1_5 /; year_5_10 / year_10_20 /; year_20+ | Residence-duration bands: under one year; 1-5; 5-10; 10-20; 20+ years. Applicable to residents; all are zero for visitors. Exact boundary inclusivity is not given in the class codebook.
Under_10_years /; Upper_10_years | Aggregated residence-duration indicators. Under_10_years combines the first three duration bands; Upper_10_years combines the last two. Both are zero for visitors.



Variables: familiarity and supporting files
-------------------------------------------
Name | Description / coding
everyday /; 2_3_days_per_week /; 1_day_per_week /; 2_3_days_per_month /; 1_month | Visitor-frequency indicators: every day; 2-3 days/week; 1 day/week; 2-3 days/month; approximately monthly. Auxiliary street-level CSV and GIS data
---------------------------------------
The folder GIS_for_street_data contains street_data_kiyosumi.csv (258 rows) and street_data_kiba.csv (473 rows), each with 43 columns. These are one-row-per-street tables, not additional person-level choices. Link them to the main sample using (city, street). Shapefiles are supplied under gis/kiyosumi_shp and gis/kiba_shp; geopandas_vis.py gives an example of visualization, with paths that may require adjustment.

Auxiliary fields | Meaning / use
street, city; distance and; visual variables | Street identifiers and attributes overlapping the main sample. Use the columns from one source consistently when merging.
included / not_included /; incl_prob | Counts of inclusion / non-inclusion among the original 50 respondents in that city, and the resulting inclusion share. These are aggregated outcomes, not street characteristics known independently of responses.
inner_river_1 to _3;; inner_avenue_1 / _2 | Additional boundary indicators. Their precise spatial definitions require the original GIS layers or study documentation.
cos_simil100/250/500;; euclid_dist100/250/500 | Additional similarity/distance variants. The suffix definitions are not specified in the class codebook; do not assume units or reference areas from the names alone.
total / extra;; cluster / cluster_type | Additional image-processing fields and streetscape cluster labels. Definitions and cluster-label mapping require the source processing documentation.
betweenness_centrality | Stored street-network centrality measure; check its normalization before comparing with independently computed centrality.
u_osmid / v_osmid;; u_lat / u_lon /; v_lat / v_lon | Identifiers and geographic coordinates of street endpoints.
u_angle / v_angle /; ave_angle | Stored endpoint/average angle measures. Confirm the reference direction and units before directional modeling.

Do not substitute the auxiliary included count for the main binary included outcome. Using incl_prob as a predictor can leak responses from the original survey into the model, including evaluation data.

Preparation notes
-----------------
Use a composite respondent ID before fitting panel models or splitting the data by person. Retain city in all street joins. Age, gender and familiarity indicators are redundant within their groups; omit a reference category when fitting an intercept. Residence-duration zeros for visitors and visit-frequency zeros for residents are structural, not evidence of zero familiarity.

For spatial models, construct adjacency or distance weights from the relevant city network and align the matrix with street IDs explicitly. The sampled rows do not form a complete person-by-street grid; preserve the observed-row mask rather than filling absent judgments with zero.



Research applications and sources
---------------------------------
A binary logit can relate neighborhood inclusion to distance, boundaries and streetscape composition. Mixed or hierarchical models can represent differences between respondents and study areas. Spatial dependence among neighboring streets can be incorporated using the methods in [2]. Because visual percentages sum to 100, including all five components together with an intercept creates exact collinearity.
